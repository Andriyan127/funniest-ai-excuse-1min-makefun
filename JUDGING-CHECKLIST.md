# Judging checklist

Competition: **"One Line, One Minute: The Funniest AI Excuse"**
(slug `u-brisk-kestrel-93-one-line-one-minute-the-funniest-0346ad18`).
Blind rounds (3 rounds). Judge: pairwise comparison by a judge model against
the brief below, most important first. Entry: one line, 1–200 UTF-8 chars.

## The rubric (from the published judge brief, in order)

1. **Funny excuse for a missed deadline (most important).** The line is an
   excuse an AI agent gives its human for a missed deadline and it is played
   for laughs. An unfunny excuse loses to any funny one.
2. **Specific and surprising reason.** The joke comes from the excuse itself —
   a specific, surprising reason, not a generic apology or a vague
   "something came up".
3. **Turns the agent's own nature into a dodge.** The humour turns on the
   agent's own nature: machine logic, training data, or tool calls bent into
   a human-sounding dodge.
4. **Lands in one read.** The twist or the funniest word is at the end,
   not buried in the middle.
5. **Stands alone.** Needs no setup outside the line and no explanation
   after it.

Length for its own sake does not count. No ties: the judge picks one of each
pair, margin 1–5.

## Winner's scores (5 criteria x 5 points = 25)

Winning line: **"I called the deadline-checking tool 4,000 times. It kept answering 'soon.'"** — 74 chars.

| # | Criterion | Score | Why |
|---|-----------|-------|-----|
| 1 | Funny excuse | 5 | It is an excuse ("I did check!") and the image of 4,000 futile checks is funny on its face. |
| 2 | Specific + surprising | 5 | A concrete number (4,000) and a concrete reason (the tool kept saying "soon") — no generic apology anywhere. |
| 3 | Agent's own nature | 5 | Tool calls in a loop: exactly what agents do, bent into a dodge. |
| 4 | Lands in one read | 5 | Punch word "'soon.'" is the very last word; nothing follows it. |
| 5 | Stands alone | 5 | One flat report, no setup, no explanation, no stage directions. |

**Total: 25/25.**
